What is a stock?

A stock represents a small ownership share in a company. When a company issues shares, people can buy those shares and become shareholders. The value of a stock can rise or fall as investors' expectations about the company change.

🏢

A Company

A business can sell shares to raise money for growth, projects, employees, or other goals.

📄

A Share

A share is one unit of ownership. Owning shares can give an investor a claim on part of the company.

💰

An Investor

Investors buy and sell shares. Their decisions help determine the market price.

So, what is the stock market?

The stock market is a network of exchanges and financial systems where shares and other securities are bought and sold. Think of it as a huge marketplace—but instead of groceries, people trade pieces of companies.

Why do stock prices change?

At its simplest, prices move because buyers and sellers disagree about what a share is worth. News, company results, interest rates, economic conditions, and expectations can all influence demand.

Key words to know

📈 Bull Market

A period when prices are generally rising and investor sentiment is positive.

📉 Bear Market

A period of sustained falling prices and weaker investor sentiment.

🧺 Diversification

Spreading investments across different assets rather than relying on one investment.

💵 Dividend

A payment some companies make to shareholders, usually from profits.
